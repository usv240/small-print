Small Print

Everyone deserves to read the small print.

After about 40, the eye's lens stiffens and close-up text blurs. Most people end up holding their phone at arm's length. Ready-made reading glasses fix this for a few dollars, but picking the right strength usually means guessing in front of a shop rack.

Small Print is a free, two-minute test in your phone's browser. The camera measures how far your face is from the screen, on your device only, so the test symbols are always drawn at the right size. It then tells you which strength to look for and lets you check a pair at the rack before you buy.

Why it matters

  • An estimated 826 million people had near-vision impairment because their presbyopia was uncorrected (Fricke et al., Ophthalmology, 2018).
  • Among people over 50, properly corrected near vision ranges from about 65% in high-income countries to under 2% in sub-Saharan Africa (Community Eye Health Journal, 2024).
  • When tea pickers in India were given reading glasses, their productivity rose 22% (PROSPER trial, Lancet Global Health, 2018). In Bangladesh, reading glasses raised income by a third for people in near-vision work (THRIVE trial, PLOS ONE, 2024). These trials measured the effect of the glasses, not of this app.

Small Print helps you choose non-prescription reading glasses. It is not an eye exam and does not detect eye disease. If your vision changes suddenly, or you have eye pain, diabetes, or glaucoma in your family, see an eye-care professional.
