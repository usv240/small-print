Safety and scope

What Small Print is for, and what it is not

Small Print helps adults choose a strength of ready-made (non-prescription) reading glasses. It is a better version of the little chart on the shop rack. It is not an eye exam, and it cannot detect eye disease.

Scope

  • For: adults whose close-up vision has become blurry with age (presbyopia) and who want to know which strength of ready-made reading glasses to try.
  • Not for: checking eye health, distance glasses, astigmatism, children, or deciding whether you need an eye exam. Home vision tests are not a substitute for a professional eye exam.12
  • Regular eye exams still matter. Getting the right readers is not a reason to skip one.12

Who should see an eye-care professional instead

Before any measuring, the test asks six yes/no questions. These are the exact questions, and what happens when the answer is yes.

Question in the appIf yes
Has your eyesight changed suddenly, or do you see flashes, a curtain or shadows?The test stops. "Please see an eye-care professional." Nothing is measured or recorded.
Are your eyes painful or red?The test stops. Same message. Nothing is measured or recorded.
Do you have diabetes?"An eye exam is a good idea." You may continue to learn an approximate strength. The result carries the same warning, and the screening is counted as a referral.
Does anyone in your family have glaucoma?
Are far-away things blurry too, like signs or faces across a room?
Do you wear glasses or contact lenses for seeing far away?

Why these questions:

  • Sudden changes, flashes, a curtain or shadow, pain and redness can be signs of a problem that needs prompt care. Reading glasses will not fix them, so the test does not continue.
  • The American Academy of Ophthalmology advises that home vision tests are not appropriate for people with conditions such as diabetes or a family history of glaucoma.12
  • Reading glasses do not correct distance blur. If far-away things are blurry, or you already wear distance glasses, an eye-care professional can tell you whether ready-made readers on their own are the right choice.

Small Print never suggests a starting strength outside +1.00 to +3.00, the range most people with presbyopia need.5 In the try-on check, you can test any pair you have in hand (+0.75 to +3.50), and the app suggests the next step up or down from that pair. The exact rules are on How it works.

Every result screen ends with the same note: this is a starting point for choosing ready-made readers, not a prescription, and if reading still feels hard, see an eye-care professional.

Why ready-made readers only

Choosing ready-made readers is a decision people already make on their own at a shop rack, and the World Health Organization's Training in Assistive Products includes a community screening method (from VisionSpring) through which trained health workers provide reading glasses outside a doctor's office.11

Determining a prescription is different. In 2017 the US Food and Drug Administration sent a warning letter to Opternative, whose online test determined refractive prescriptions, taking the position that the test was a medical device marketed without the required clearance or approval.13 Small Print stays on the other side of that line: it does not determine a prescription, measure astigmatism or assess eye health. It helps you pick among ready-made readers. This explains how we framed the product; it is not legal advice.

Privacy: exactly what is stored

Video never leaves your device. The camera feed is processed inside your browser to measure distance, frame by frame, and then discarded. No photo, video or face measurement is uploaded. You can check this in your browser's developer tools: the only request the test makes to our server is one small JSON result (the server rejects anything over 4 KB).

When you reach a result, the browser sends one anonymous record. If you later tell us about a try-on or the readers you already own, the same record is updated (it is never counted twice). These are all the fields; the server rejects any request with extra fields.

FieldWhat it holds
vFormat version (always 1).
sessionIdA random ID created fresh for each test, so later answers update the same record. Not linked to you or your device.
trafficreal, demo, judge or dev (so test traffic is never counted as real use).
modecamera or demo (slider instead of camera).
langLanguage used: en, es, fr or pt.
devicephone, tablet or desktop (from screen size and touch input; not the model).
ageBandFive-year band (under 40, 40–44, … 60–64, 65+). Your exact age is not sent.
outcomereaders, no-readers or refer.
referReasonsWhich advisory safety answers were yes, plus the app's own flags (possible short sight, above +3.00, estimates disagree).
startStrengthThe suggested starting strength, or empty.
workingDistanceCmYour reading distance, rounded to whole centimetres.
nearPointCmYour near point, rounded to whole centimetres, or empty.
nearPointBeyondReachWhether the E stayed blurry at arm's length.
calibratedWhether the screen and the camera were calibrated (yes/no each; not the values).
tryOnUp to six pairs tried: the strength and the verdict (good, stronger, weaker).
existingReadersThe strength printed on readers you already own, only if you choose to tell us.

The server adds only: an agreement category computed from the last two fields (exact, within 0.50, further), the times the record was created and last updated, and a revision counter.

Not stored or logged by us: images, video, face landmarks, name, email, exact age, location, IP address, browser user agent, and your calibration values. API access logs leave out IP address and user agent. Server logs record only validated values and error types, never request bodies. CloudFront and S3 access logs are switched off because they would record visitors' IP addresses.

Kept in your browser only: your screen and camera calibration, language and voice on/off (local storage), and the judge flag for the current tab (session storage). Clearing this site's data removes them; opening the test with ?recalibrate clears the calibration.

Published: only totals across all screenings, on the impact page. Individual records are never published.

Retention: results are kept in one database table with point-in-time recovery. There is no automatic expiry yet. Because nothing in a record identifies you, we also cannot find "your" record to delete it.

Accessibility

  • No reading needed for the test itself. The tumbling E is answered by direction: swipe, tap an arrow button, or press an arrow key.
  • Voice guide. Each step is spoken. The clips are pre-generated with Amazon Polly; if a clip is missing, the browser's own speech voice reads the text. Voice can be switched off.
  • Languages. English, Spanish, French and Portuguese can be chosen; any text not yet translated falls back to English. Strengths use the local decimal separator (+1,50).
  • Large type, high contrast, large buttons, and a dark theme that follows your device setting.
  • Screen readers and keyboards. Each step moves focus to its heading; the live distance and progress messages are announced politely; every control is a real button or form field.
  • No camera, or camera blocked? A demo mode replaces the camera with a slider, so anyone can see how the test works.

Limits we know about: the test still needs enough sight to see a phone screen and enough light for the camera to find your eyes. We have not yet run sessions with screen-reader users or with people who cannot read, so we don't claim the flow works well for them; that is part of the proposed pilot.
