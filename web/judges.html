Try it in 60 seconds

Three ways in: on your phone (about two minutes, a real result for your own eyes), on a laptop webcam, or with no camera at all (60 seconds, a scripted result you can check). Every step says what you should see.

Links with ?judge mark your tries as judge traffic, so they are counted separately and never mixed with real use.

Watch first (86 seconds)

A. On your phone (recommended)

On a desktop? Open this address on your phone:

https://dxug72099q2ay.cloudfront.net/test.html?judge

Use Chrome or Safari. Browsers built into social and messaging apps often block the camera; if the camera won't start, open the link in your phone's main browser.

  1. Open the link and tap "Start with camera". "First, a few safety questions": six yes/no questions.
  2. Answer honestly and tap Continue. If you answer yes to diabetes, glaucoma in the family, distance blur or distance glasses, you'll see "An eye exam is a good idea"; tap "Continue anyway". "How old are you?" with − and + buttons.
  3. Set your age, tap Continue, then "Allow camera" and accept the browser's prompt. "Match a card to the screen" (first visit on this device only).
  4. Hold any bank, ID or library card against the screen, drag the slider until the box is exactly as wide, and tap "It matches". No card? Tap "I don't have a card". "Hold the screen 30 cm from your eyes", with a live distance pill (for example "31 cm") at the top that changes as you move.
  5. Hold the screen 30 cm from your eyes (a ruler, or the long side of an A4 sheet) and tap "It's 30 cm now". Or tap "Skip (less accurate)". "Hold your phone where you'd like to read". The pill tracks your distance live.
  6. Hold the phone where you'd comfortably read, without stretching, and keep still. A bar fills in about two seconds, then "Got it: you read at about … cm". Tap Continue.
  7. Swipe, or tap the arrow, in the direction the E points. Five times. Guess if unsure. "1 of 5" … "5 of 5", then "Is this E sharp?"
  8. Tap Sharp or Blurry, then move the phone slowly as asked and tap the moment the E changes. The E gets physically bigger as you move away and smaller as you come closer, so it always looks the same size to you. That is the constant-angle letter.
  9. Read your result. "Your result": a starting strength from +1.00 to +3.00 and which pair to try first (or "You may not need reading glasses yet", or a referral), plus "Just for fun: your eyes focus like a typical …-year-old's". Open "How we worked this out" to see the numbers behind it.

B. On a laptop webcam

Open the same address in Chrome or Edge on your laptop: /test.html?judge. The steps are identical. Instead of moving the screen, lean towards and away from it. Calibration works the same way: the card against the screen, then your eyes 30 cm from the screen.

The same live distance pill, the same resizing E, and a result card.

C. No camera: a scripted 60-second run

A slider stands in for the camera, so the whole flow runs anywhere. This script has a known answer you can check against the worked example.

https://dxug72099q2ay.cloudfront.net/test.html?demo&judge
  1. Open the link. The safety questions, with a "Distance from your eyes" slider at the bottom and the note "Demo mode: the slider stands in for the camera."
  2. Answer No to all six questions and tap Continue. "How old are you?", showing 50.
  3. Tap + twice to set 52 and tap Continue. "Hold your phone where you'd like to read".
  4. Set the slider to 38 cm and leave it. The bar fills, then "Got it: you read at about 38 cm." Tap Continue.
  5. Answer the five E's with the arrows (any answers work for this script). "Is this E sharp?"
  6. Tap Blurry, move the slider to 60 cm, and tap "Now it's sharp". As the slider moves out, the E grows on screen.
  7. Read the result. "Start with reading glasses of +2.00", "At the shop, try +2.00 first.", and "Just for fun: your eyes focus like a typical 57-year-old's."
  8. Optional: camp mode for health workers. Open /test.html?camp&demo&judge and finish one person as above, then tap Next person and finish a second. Then tap Camp summary. A tally of people screened, a table of strengths, and "Pairs to bring for the next 100 people" (for two people at +2.00: +2.00: 101), with a CSV download. Each person is sent as a separate anonymous session.
  9. Optional: the rack poster. Open /poster.html, switch the language, and use your browser's print preview. An A4 poster with a QR code that opens the test, the four steps, and the safety note, in the chosen language.
  10. Optional: the try-on check. Tap "Check a pair in the shop", pick the strength printed on a pair (here +2.00 is preselected), tap Continue, and follow the screens: find a sharp spot, move closer until it blurs, then away until it blurs. A verdict ("Good fit", "Too weak … try …" or "Too strong … try …") and a bar showing your sharp range and your reading distance. For example, with +2.00, marking 25 cm then 60 cm gives "Good fit. These +2.00 glasses suit your reading distance."

Tested on

  • Google Pixel phone, Chrome on Android.
  • Windows laptop webcam, Chrome and Edge.
  • iPhone Safari: best effort. We have not tested on an iPhone; the no-camera demo works in any modern browser.

If something goes wrong

  • "Can't see your face." Face the screen in good light, about arm's length or closer.
  • The camera won't start. Open the link in Chrome or Safari, not inside another app, and allow camera access. The camera screen also offers "Try without a camera".
  • Redo the calibration (for example on a shared device): /test.html?judge&recalibrate.
  • Small Print is not an eye exam. Your result is a starting point for choosing ready-made readers. See Safety and scope.
