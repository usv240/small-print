How it works

Three steps, about two minutes

  1. The camera measures distance, live

    The coloured part of the eye (the iris) is about the same size in every adult, so its size on camera tells us how far away your face is. This runs entirely in your browser; video never leaves the phone.

  2. The test resizes itself, and needs no reading

    A letter E is redrawn about 30 times a second so it always looks exactly the same size to your eye, wherever you hold the phone. You swipe in the direction it points, with a voice guide in English, Spanish, French or Portuguese.

  3. Your strength, then a check of the pair in the shop

    It uses your own reading distance: a tailor holding work at 28 cm needs a different strength from someone reading at 40 cm. Put a pair on and the test checks that your reading distance sits in the middle of your sharp range (the standard rule eye-care workers use), or tells you to go stronger or weaker.

The problem with rack cards

Why choosing reading glasses goes wrong

The usual way to choose is a little chart on the shop rack, or a printable one online. It only works if all three of these are true, and in practice they rarely are.

It must be held at exactly the right distance

Usually 14 inches (35.6 cm). Hold it at 30 cm instead and the result is off by about half a dioptre, a full strength step.see the maths

It must be printed or shown at exactly the right size

Browsers don’t know how big a screen pixel physically is, so an un-calibrated on-screen chart can draw its letters at the wrong size. Why, and how we fix it.

You have to be able to read it

In one of the trials above, only 35% of participants could read.4

For organisations

For pharmacies, clinics and health workers

Camp mode

Try camp mode

Screen a queue of people on one phone. Each person is anonymous; the phone keeps a tally and tells you how many pairs of each strength to bring for the next 100 people. After the first visit it works offline, and results are sent when the signal returns.

Rack poster

Print the poster

A free printable A4 poster with a QR code for the reading-glasses rack, in four languages. Customers scan it, test, and check the pair before they buy.

Trust

Built to be trusted

Private by design

No account, no name, no images. We store only an anonymous result (age band, strength, distance) so the impact dashboard can count screenings.

Safe by default

Warning signs like sudden changes, pain, or diabetes send people to an eye-care professional instead of a shop. Anything beyond the +3.00 range of ready-made readers is referred. Scope and limits.

No AI guessing about your eyes

The recommendation is deterministic optics from published sources, so it can be tested and checked. We test it on thousands of simulated eyes and publish where it fails.

Honest about what’s proven

Small Print has not yet been validated in a field study. We say exactly what we measured, what we simulated, and what still needs a pilot. Validation.
